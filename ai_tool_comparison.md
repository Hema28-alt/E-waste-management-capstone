AI Tool Comparison Report

1. Project Title

AI Responsible Electronics: Standards, Safety and E-Waste Research

2. AI Tools Used

- ChatGPT
- Google Gemini

3. Research Method

The same research questions about India's E-Waste (Management) Rules, Extended Producer Responsibility (EPR), registered recyclers, environmental and health risks, and official verification sources were submitted to both AI tools.

The responses were compared for agreement, detail, source quality and claims requiring further verification.

4. Comparison Table

5. Similarities

1. Both tools gave 1 April 2023 as the commencement date.
2. Both explained that producers have EPR obligations.
3. Both described registered recyclers as important to e-waste recycling and EPR certificates.
4. Both identified health and environmental risks from improper e-waste handling.
5. Both recommended official government sources for verification.

6. Differences

1. Gemini provided a longer explanation of EPR and recycler operations.
2. Gemini named specific hazardous substances and possible health effects.
3. ChatGPT gave a shorter explanation and emphasized checking current rules and amendments.
4. Both responses require checking the supporting evidence, not just comparing the wording.

7. Verification and Reliability Findings

- The commencement date of 1 April 2023 is supported by official government information.
- The CPCB E-Waste EPR Portal explains that producers fulfil their EPR obligations through certificates from registered recyclers.
- The Ministry's current rules page lists the original Rules and amendments.
- Gemini's specific claims about individual chemicals, organ damage and increased cancer risk need separate authoritative scientific sources before they can be treated as verified.
- Claims about all end-of-life products avoiding landfills or informal scrap markets should not be treated as guaranteed outcomes without supporting evidence.
- The screenshots show government portal names, but do not establish that every individual claim was verified against a particular rule, page or scientific source.

9. Limitations

This comparison uses the responses available during the project. The screenshots do not show a complete source-by-source verification of every claim. Specific scientific and legal claims require further checking against authoritative sources. AI answers may be incorrect, incomplete or outdated and are not legal advice.

10. Conclusion

ChatGPT and Gemini produced similar answers to the main research questions, especially the commencement date and the role of EPR. Gemini provided more detail, but additional detail is not automatically more reliable. The comparison demonstrates why AI-generated claims must be checked against official legal documents and appropriate scientific sources before publication.
