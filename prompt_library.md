AI Prompt Library — E-Waste Capstone

1. Project Information

Project title: AI Responsible Electronics: Standards, Safety and E-Waste Research

Project number: 23

Purpose: To investigate how AI can support student research on electronics safety and e-waste rules, and to evaluate the reliability of AI-generated legal and technical information.

2. Main Research Prompt

Prompt 1 — AI tool comparison

«Explain India's E-Waste (Management) Rules, 2022 in simple language. Include the notification date, effective date, Extended Producer Responsibility (EPR), the role of registered recyclers, and possible health and environmental risks from unsafe e-waste handling. Identify official sources that can be used to verify the information. Clearly mention any uncertainty and avoid unsupported claims.»

Tools: ChatGPT and Google Gemini.

Purpose: To compare how two AI tools explain the same research topic.

3. Supporting Research Prompts

Prompt 2 — Understanding legal terminology

«Explain Extended Producer Responsibility (EPR) in India's e-waste management system in simple language. Describe the general responsibilities of producers and the role of registered recyclers. Identify official sources for checking the explanation.»

Purpose: To understand technical and legal terminology.

Prompt 3 — Claim verification

«Review the following claims about India's E-Waste (Management) Rules, 2022: the notification date, effective date, producer EPR obligations, and the role of registered recyclers. For each claim, identify a relevant official source, state what the source supports, and mark any claim that still requires verification. Do not invent citations.»

Purpose: To distinguish verified information from uncertain claims.

Prompt 4 — Environmental awareness article

«Draft an educational article for college students about e-waste. Explain what e-waste is, why unsafe handling is a concern, how to reduce and recycle it responsibly, the basic purpose of India's e-waste rules, and how AI can assist research. Use simple English and recommend official sources for legal information. Avoid unsupported scientific or legal claims.»

Purpose: To prepare student-friendly awareness content.

Prompt 5 — Social media awareness

«Create five short social media awareness posts about e-waste reduction, repair and reuse, responsible recycling, India's e-waste rules, and responsible AI research. Give each post a clear heading, a practical message, and relevant hashtags. Do not claim that the posts have been published.»

Purpose: To develop awareness campaign materials.

Prompt 6 — Presentation outline

«Prepare a six-slide presentation outline for a college capstone on AI and e-waste research. Include the problem, objectives, research method, comparison of ChatGPT and Gemini, official-source verification, limitations, and conclusion. Keep the points concise and suitable for a student presentation.»

Purpose: To plan the project presentation.

Prompt 7 — Responsible AI evaluation

«Identify possible limitations when students use AI tools to research laws and technical topics. Explain why answers from two AI tools may still be incorrect, how official sources can help verify claims, and how students should report uncertainty honestly.»

Purpose: To analyse AI reliability and responsible use.

4. Prompt Engineering Techniques

The prompts use several useful techniques:

1. Role and audience clarity: The content is designed for students and college projects.
2. Context setting: Prompts specify Indian e-waste rules and the project's research question.
3. Task decomposition: Large topics are divided into dates, EPR, recycler responsibilities, risks and verification.
4. Output requirements: Tables, headings, short posts and slide outlines make results easier to evaluate.
5. Evidence requests: Prompts ask for official sources and clear uncertainty.
6. Responsible constraints: Prompts instruct the AI not to invent citations or present unsupported claims as facts.
7. Comparative evaluation: The same main research prompt is used with ChatGPT and Google Gemini.

5. Prompt Evaluation

Evaluation criterion| What to check
Relevance| Does the response answer the research question?
Accuracy| Are legal dates and obligations supported by official sources?
Evidence| Are sources named and linked clearly?
Completeness| Are important parts of the prompt addressed?
Clarity| Can a college student understand the answer?
Uncertainty| Does the response identify unsupported or unverified claims?
Responsible use| Does it avoid fabricated citations and misleading certainty?

6. Limitations

The main comparison used ChatGPT and Google Gemini to examine the same topic. The supporting prompts in this library provide a reproducible workflow for further research and content creation. They should not be interpreted as proof that every supporting prompt was separately executed or that every generated claim was verified.

The prompts can be reused, but their results must be checked against current official information before submission or publication.

7. Conclusion

A well-written prompt helps an AI tool produce relevant and organized information. However, prompt quality alone does not guarantee accuracy. Combining clear instructions, comparison between tools, official-source verification and honest reporting of limitations makes AI-assisted research more responsible and useful.
