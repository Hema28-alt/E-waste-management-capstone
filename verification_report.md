Verification Report: AI-Generated E-Waste Information

1. Purpose

This report evaluates the reliability of information generated by ChatGPT and Google Gemini for the project "AI Responsible Electronics: Standards, Safety and E-Waste Research."

The purpose is to identify important claims, compare AI responses, check them against official sources and record any limitations.

2. Verification Method

The following steps were used:

1. Ask ChatGPT and Google Gemini the same main research question.
2. Compare their explanations and identify common claims.
3. Identify claims that require legal or technical verification.
4. Consult official sources, especially the Central Pollution Control Board (CPCB).
5. Record whether each claim is supported, partly supported or requires further checking.
6. Avoid treating agreement between two AI tools as proof of accuracy.

5. Comparison of AI Responses

ChatGPT and Google Gemini provided similar introductory explanations of the rules, their effective date and the general concept of EPR.

However, similar responses do not necessarily establish that all details are correct. AI tools may omit conditions, use outdated information or make claims without adequate evidence.

The responses and screenshots collected for this project did not establish complete source-by-source verification for every statement. Therefore, the comparison is useful for identifying claims to check, rather than proving that one tool is more accurate than the other.

6. Errors, Risks and Uncertainties

The following risks were identified:

- Outdated information: Legal rules may be amended after an AI tool's information was collected.
- Missing conditions: A simplified explanation may omit important requirements or exceptions.
- Unsupported claims: An AI response may make a technical or health-related claim without sufficient evidence.
- False confidence: A well-written answer may still contain errors.
- Citation limitations: Mentioning an official website does not automatically prove every statement in the answer.

Important claims should be checked against the relevant official document or a suitable scientific source.

7. Improvements Recommended

1. Include the date and jurisdiction in research prompts.
2. Ask AI tools to identify official sources for legal claims.
3. Open the cited source and check the relevant information directly.
4. Compare AI responses without assuming that agreement means correctness.
5. Mark claims as supported, partly supported, unverified or contradicted.
6. Use scientific publications or authoritative technical sources for detailed health and engineering claims.
7. Record limitations and avoid inventing accuracy percentages or test results.

8. Conclusion

The verification exercise supports the main legal points about the effective date of the E-Waste (Management) Rules, 2022, and the general EPR framework. It also demonstrates that AI-generated explanations require careful checking.

AI can assist students with preliminary research, but it should not replace official documents, scientific evidence or human judgment. Responsible AI-assisted research depends on reliable references, transparent uncertainty and honest reporting.
