Presentation: AI Responsible Electronics — Standards, Safety and E-Waste Research

Slide 1 — Title

AI Responsible Electronics: Standards, Safety and E-Waste Research

- Capstone Project No. 23
- Department: Electronics and Communication Engineering
- Focus: AI-assisted research, e-waste awareness and information verification

Opening statement:
"This project explores how AI tools can help students understand e-waste management and why their answers must be verified."

Slide 2 — Problem Statement and Objectives

Problem statement

Electronic waste requires responsible handling and recycling. Students may find legal and technical information difficult to understand, while AI tools can sometimes provide incomplete or inaccurate information.

Objectives

- Understand India's E-Waste (Management) Rules, 2022.
- Explore how AI can support student research.
- Compare ChatGPT and Google Gemini.
- Verify important claims using official sources.
- Develop awareness materials for students.

Slide 3 — AI Tools and Research Workflow

AI tools explored

- ChatGPT — initial research and content drafting.
- Google Gemini — comparison of explanations.

Research workflow

1. Define the research question.
2. Ask both AI tools the same main prompt.
3. Compare their responses.
4. Check key claims against official sources.
5. Record uncertainties and prepare awareness content.

Key point: AI generates a starting point; it does not replace verification.

Slide 4 — Main Research Findings

- India's E-Waste (Management) Rules, 2022 came into effect on 1 April 2023.
- Extended Producer Responsibility (EPR) establishes specified responsibilities for producers.
- Registered recyclers play an important role in the prescribed recycling and certificate system.
- Unsafe e-waste handling can pose risks to human health and the environment.
- Repair, reuse and responsible recycling can help reduce avoidable electronic waste.

Official sources: CPCB and the Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change.

Slide 5 — Verification and Responsible AI

What the comparison showed

- Both tools provided introductory explanations of e-waste rules and EPR.
- Similar answers did not prove that every detail was correct.
- Official sources supported the key legal dates and general EPR framework.
- Detailed technical and health-related claims require appropriate evidence.

Responsible AI practices

- Check official sources.
- Avoid unsupported claims.
- Record references.
- Report uncertainty honestly.
- Do not invent research results or accuracy percentages.

Slide 6 — Conclusion and Future Scope

Conclusion

AI tools can simplify research, organize information and help create awareness materials. Reliable results still require official-source verification and human judgment.

Future scope

- Develop a student e-waste awareness campaign.
- Create a poster or infographic.
- Prepare a short demonstration of the verification workflow.
- Expand research using additional authoritative technical sources.

Closing statement:
"Use AI to assist research, verify important facts, and manage e-waste responsibly."
