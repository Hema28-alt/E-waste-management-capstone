Before-and-After Analysis: AI-Assisted E-Waste Research

1. Objective

The objective of this analysis is to examine how AI tools can help students organize information about e-waste management and why human verification is necessary to improve reliability.

2. Before Using AI

Before using AI tools, a student researching e-waste may face these challenges:

- Legal terms such as Extended Producer Responsibility (EPR) may be difficult to understand.
- Information about rules and recycling may be spread across several documents.
- Preparing an awareness article and social media posts may take more time.
- It may be difficult to distinguish an official source from an unsupported online claim.
- Research findings may lack a clear structure.

These are potential challenges identified for this project, not results from a formal student survey.

3. After Using AI

ChatGPT and Google Gemini were used to explore the same main research question.

AI-assisted research helped to:

- Explain e-waste terminology in simpler language.
- Organize information into headings and key points.
- Identify relevant topics, including EPR, registered recyclers and environmental concerns.
- Prepare drafts of awareness materials.
- Develop a repeatable set of research and verification prompts.

The outputs still required checking. AI-generated information was not treated as automatically correct.

4. Before-and-After Comparison

Criterion| Before AI assistance| After AI assistance
Understanding terminology| May require reading several technical explanations| AI can provide a simpler initial explanation
Organization| Notes may be scattered| Information can be grouped under headings
Content preparation| Article and awareness-post drafting may take longer| AI can generate editable first drafts
Source verification| Requires manually finding relevant references| AI can suggest sources, which must then be checked
Reliability| Depends on the sources consulted| Still depends on evidence and verification
Final responsibility| Student must assess information| Student must still assess information and correct errors

5. Verification Example

Claim: India's E-Waste (Management) Rules, 2022 came into effect on 1 April 2023.

AI-assisted stage: ChatGPT and Google Gemini provided the date in their explanations.

Verification stage: The date was checked against the Central Pollution Control Board's official e-waste FAQ.

Finding: The official FAQ supports the effective date.

Official reference:
https://cpcb.nic.in/uploads/Projects/E-Waste/FAQ_ewaste_23012024.pdf

This example shows why a claim should be checked against an authoritative source rather than accepted only because an AI tool provides it.

6. Limitations of the Analysis

This project used a small comparison of two AI tools. It did not measure the exact time saved, conduct a formal user survey, or calculate an accuracy percentage. Therefore, it would not be appropriate to claim a specific percentage improvement or state that AI was more accurate than official sources.

The comparison focuses on the usefulness, clarity, organization and verification needs of AI-assisted research.

7. Lessons Learned

1. Clear prompts help AI produce more relevant and organized answers.
2. Using two AI tools can help identify common explanations and differences.
3. Agreement between AI tools does not prove that a claim is correct.
4. Official sources are essential when checking legal dates and requirements.
5. AI-generated awareness material should be reviewed before publication.
6. Students should report limitations honestly and avoid inventing research results.

8. Conclusion

AI tools can support early-stage research by simplifying terminology, organizing information and preparing content drafts. In this project, ChatGPT and Google Gemini helped explore India's e-waste management framework. Official-source checking supported key legal information, while other technical claims still required appropriate evidence.

The main lesson is that AI can make research more convenient, but reliable research requires human judgment, authoritative sources and transparent reporting.
